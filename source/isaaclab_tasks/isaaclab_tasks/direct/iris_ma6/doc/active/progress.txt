    (0be8: -0.64 vs 5ff6: -0.09 at step 20k)
  - Safety/collision rate also 2-3x higher in 24D runs at step 2k (0.55 vs 0.26)

    causing erratic early exploration that triggers more collisions

----------------------------------------

----------------------------------------
Done:
- Diagnosed second-validation run (962be96b48 MEAN-fix). It does not
  collapse like the SUM run did, but degrades to ~half pre-fix's late-

- Codex flagged the structural mismatch: stock SKRL uses the same
  numeric `kl_threshold` value for both PPO early-stop and KLAdaptive's
  target. With default kl_factor=2 the bands are: PPO-stop at 0.02,
  KLAdaptive-lower at 0.04. KL ~0.023 sits in [0.02, 0.04] where PPO
  early-stops but KLAdaptive is inert — LR keeps climbing while updates
  are intermittent. Confirmed by reading SKRL source: the conflation of
  the two thresholds is structural in stock SKRL, not a bug introduced
  by my fix.
- Principled answer: PPO literature (Schulman 2017, SpinningUp, SB3)
  uses `1.5 × target_kl` for early-stop, just inside KLAdaptive's high
  band of `2 × target_kl`. SKRL's convention of `1.0 × target_kl` is
  non-standard and creates the inert region.
- Applied cfg-side band alignment in agents/skrl_mappo_rnn_cfg.yaml
  (no trainer code changes):
    agent.kl_threshold: 0.02         (PPO early-stop, unchanged)
    scheduler.kl_threshold: 0.01     (NEW: target = early-stop / 2)
    scheduler.kl_factor: 2.0         (NEW: pinned explicit)
    scheduler.min_lr: 1.0e-4         (unchanged)
    scheduler.max_lr: 1.0e-3         (NEW: cap to prevent runaway)
  This makes scheduler_high = target × kl_factor = 0.02 = PPO-stop, so
  early-stop and LR-decrease fire together at KL=0.02 instead of having
  an inert region between them. max_lr=1e-3 caps LR at the level that
  pre-fix's drone_0 averaged (was reaching 0.005-0.008 in the broken
  post-fix run). YAML carries an explicit comment block describing the
  band layout and warning operators not to break the 2:1 ratio between
  the two thresholds.

  `target × factor == early_stop` (0.02 = 0.02). OK.

HEADLINE FINDING:
  In Pegasus lockstep SITL, the consumer-side EKF-channel lag is ≤10 ms
  (below the 5 ms 200 Hz measurement resolution) on position, velocity
  (world & body), and attitude_yaw. attitude_pitch reports 16.7 ms.

  Two mechanisms together produce this:

  (1) PX4 EKF lag-compensation via OutputPredictor. PX4 runs the Kalman
  filter at a delayed-fusion horizon (110 ms behind for GPS-driven
  channels), but integrates raw IMU forward to t=NOW via
  OutputPredictor.calculateOutputStates() and publishes the predicted
  state-at-NOW with timestamp=NOW. Source: PX4-Autopilot
  src/modules/ekf2/EKF/output_predictor.cpp:168 and
  EKF2.cpp:1410 PublishLocalPosition(now).

  (2) Lockstep + use_sim_time collapses the publish-rate freshness gap.
  With /clock paused between simulator ticks, no sim-time elapses
  between PX4 emit and recorder receive. Median (recv_stamp_s -
  Header.stamp) is exactly 0 ms on every topic. In wall-clock real-time
  a 30 Hz mavros publisher would give ~17 ms median freshness gap; in
  lockstep that's below the /clock tick.

  Combined, my Header.stamp-aligned xcorr captures BOTH estimation lag
  AND freshness in lockstep SITL (because stamp-time and recv-time are
  the same time-base here). The 0 ms result therefore IS the
  consumer-experienced lag, FOR THIS SETUP ONLY.

  IMPORTANT LIMITATION: this number does not generalize to real
  hardware. Real deployment runs without lockstep (/clock = wall-time),
  mavros publishes at ~30 Hz over MAVLink-serial (~5-10 ms transport),
  so the consumer will see a 15-25 ms median freshness gap plus any
  estimation-lag degradation from real sensor noise. SITL is the
  load-bearing floor; real-hardware re-measurement is a follow-up
  ticket and must use wall-clock subscribers (no use_sim_time) so
  freshness is visible in recv-stamp.
