## 1. Hypothesis

Both 949d and 492f collapsed at ~100k when FP/FN onset hit because the policy had never
encountered bbox-empty states. Sigma was too low (0.44 and 0.48 respectively) to adapt on
the fly. The intermediate runs (d60d, fe720bf) changed too many variables simultaneously
(min_log_std, entropy coefficient, curriculum compression, dynamics DR timing), making
results unattributable.

This run reverts entirely to the validated 492f configuration and adds a single targeted
fix: **FP/FN background exposure at 10% from step 15k**. The policy encounters bbox-empty
states for ~85k steps before the full FP/FN ramp, giving it a learned hold-last-position
response instead of the fragile "bbox always valid" assumption that caused 949d/492f collapse.

**Expected outcome**: Training through 100k matches 492f (healthy LR, sigma growing at
+0.004/kstep, reward ~3100). At noise/FP/FN onset (100k), the policy survives because it
has already learned to handle bbox-empty states from the background exposure.

### Key comparisons to make

1. **Bootstrap health with background FP/FN**: Does 10% FP/FN from 15k measurably
   degrade pair_valid compared to 492f's clean bootstrap? If pair_valid < 0.85 at 20k,
   the background rate may be too high.

2. **Sigma trajectory**: If sigma matches 492f (+0.004/kstep), the background FP/FN
   is not interfering with the entropy dynamics. If sigma is higher, the occasional
   bbox-empty states may be providing additional exploration pressure (good).

3. **Noise-onset survival**: The make-or-break test. 492f collapsed from pair_valid
   0.79 → 0.10 in 24k steps. If 025af4d maintains pair_valid > 0.50 through 120k,
   the background inoculation hypothesis is confirmed.

4. **Policy loss sign at noise onset**: 949d had negative policy loss (exploiting
   narrow strategy into collapse). 492f had positive policy loss (entropy growing
   but too late). A healthy response would be briefly positive then stabilizing.
