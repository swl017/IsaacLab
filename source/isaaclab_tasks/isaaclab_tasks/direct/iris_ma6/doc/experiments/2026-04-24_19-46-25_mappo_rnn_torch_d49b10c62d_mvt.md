## 1. Hypothesis (going in)

Narrowing the initial-state distribution to the easy slice and freezing
the non-observation curriculum should let the policy commit to a tight
sigma faster, then absorb the obs-corruption ramps (100k–220k) without
the late-phase ep_len collapse seen in `0d4fa`. The MVT config keeps the
three sim-to-sim-critical randomizations (cylinder Z-spread, target
height offset, orientation noise) so the simplification doesn't strip
away the variability the gimbal/yaw policy needs to remain transferable.

## 4. Analysis — Why MVT failed

### 4.1 Geometry trap: agent spawn radius overlaps target placement zone

`cylinder_diameter_max=25` puts agents within a radius of **12.5 m** of
the cylinder centre. `target_distance_min/max=10/15` puts the target
**10–15 m** from the cylinder centre. With no agent-target separation
constraint in initial-state generation, agent-target spacing can be
**0–27.5 m**, with the bulk of the distribution sitting *under* the
7 m `target_proximity_threshold`.

Confirmed in the reward decomposition:

| Metric | 0d4fa (40k) | d49b10 MVT (40k) |
|---|---:|---:|
| `target_proximity` reward | -1.96 | **-5.41** |
| `collision_fraction` | 0.0007 | **0.0029** (×4) |
| `collision` reward | -0.07 | **-0.27** |
| `altitude` reward | 0.00 | -0.05 |

The `target_proximity_penalty_scale=-50` per-metre-deficit penalty fires
on essentially every reset, costing ~5 reward/episode before the policy
takes a single useful action. The `0d4fa` baseline rarely sees this
because curriculum-controlled `cylinder_diameter_max` ramps from 20 to
100 m, so by mid-training agents are 50 m radius and the target is 40 m
away — well outside the 7 m proximity threshold.

### 4.2 Sigma is the smoking gun

| Step | 0d4fa sigma | MVT sigma |
|-----:|------------:|----------:|
| 4k   | 0.45 | 0.49 |
| 20k  | **0.14** | **0.45** |
| 80k  | 0.14 | 0.45 |
| 240k | 0.39 | 0.45 |
| 400k | 0.42 | 0.45 |

MVT sigma is essentially flat at the initialization value across the
full run. The policy never finds a strategy with positive expected
return — every direction it explores triggers proximity/collision
penalties — so the entropy bonus dominates and sigma stays maximal.

This matches the entropy-loss signal: ~0 throughout (vs `0d4fa`
sustaining +0.01–0.015 through the productive phase). Zero entropy
loss means zero policy concentration.

### 4.3 The "fixed observer" change is also load-bearing (the wrong way)

`designated_observer_mode: random → fixed` was meant to remove
credit-assignment variance — agent 0 always observer. But combined with
the proximity trap, this means **agent 0 is consistently spawned in the
worst-case proximity regime** and never benefits from rotation. The
non-observer agent has no reward gradient on tracking quality (it's
indirect via inter-agent obs), so it can't compensate. Random observer
selection in `0d4fa` averaged over this asymmetry; MVT bakes it in.

### 4.4 Slow target makes it harder, not easier

`target_max_velocity` 2.0 → 0.5 was intended to simplify tracking. But
the bbox-size reward (peak ≈20 % of image area) is *easier* with a
moving target because the agent can use lead-pursuit and zoom dynamics.
A near-static target close in (10–15 m) appears very large in image
space and the policy's zoom range (capped at zoom_max=2 in MVT,
zoom_initial=1) can't pull it small enough — bbox saturates the FOV,
`bbox_size` reward drops.

Confirmed: MVT `bbox_size` reward at 100k is 15.0 (per episode of 191
steps = 0.079/step) vs `0d4fa` 45.7 (per 496 steps = 0.092/step). MVT
bbox reward density is **lower** despite the closer target.

## 5. Decision

**MVT config does not work as written.** The narrowing collapsed the
agent-target geometry into the proximity-penalty regime and removed the
diversity that allowed the policy to find a non-degenerate strategy.

The roadmap's Phase-A premise (narrow distribution → train obs
corruption robustness) is still reasonable, but the specific narrowing
chosen pushed the distribution into the *hardest* corner instead of the
easiest. Three things must change before re-running:

1. **Push target out of the proximity zone.** Either:
   - `target_distance_min: 10 → 25`, `target_distance_max: 15 → 30` (target firmly outside the 7 m threshold for any agent placement), OR
   - Add agent-target rejection sampling in `initial_states_generator.py` enforcing `min_agent_target_distance ≥ target_proximity_threshold + clearance` (e.g., 10 m).
2. **Restore `designated_observer_mode: random`.** Removing role rotation in a 2-agent env amplified the bad-spawn problem instead of reducing variance.
3. **Re-evaluate the velocity halving.** The slow target hurt bbox-size reward. Either keep target at the original 2 m/s or widen `zoom_initial_max_end` so the policy can frame a near-static large target.

A `phase_a_mvt_v2` revision should land all three changes
simultaneously and be smoke-tested *before* a 400k run by checking that
mean `target_proximity` reward in the first 20k stays > -1.0 (vs MVT's
-8.5 at 20k).

**Open question for the user**: was the agent-target proximity
constraint (or its absence) intentional? If the assumption is that the
narrowed cylinder + narrowed target-distance jointly imply agents are
"around" the target rather than "near" it, the
`initial_states_generator` is silently violating that assumption —
worth a separate inspection of the placement code regardless of this
re-run.

- The sim-to-sim-critical kept-random axes (cylinder_height_range,
  target_height_offset, orientation_noise) are not implicated in the

- The obs-corruption curriculum was never given a fair test — the

  ablations cannot start from this run; we need a working MVT v2

- 400k of compute "wasted" but the diagnostic value is high: this run
  isolates the proximity-trap mechanism from any obs-corruption effect,
