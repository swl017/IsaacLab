## 4. Analysis

### The compressed curriculum delivers faster learning but earlier noise exposure

The tradeoff is clear:
- **Benefit**: Triangulation 35.7 at 52k (vs 26.4 at 100k in 492f) — 2x faster throughput
- **Cost**: Noise arrives at 50k when sigma is 0.40 (vs 100k when sigma was 0.48)

Sigma at noise onset is actually *lower* in the compressed schedule (0.40 at 50k vs
0.48 at 100k). The compression didn't buy sigma more growth time — it just moved
everything earlier proportionally.

### Why partial recovery is occurring (and didn't in 949d/492f)

The compressed schedule hits noise 36k steps earlier. This means:
1. The policy has seen less total training → less committed to a narrow strategy
2. The LR is higher at cliff onset (0.006 vs 0.007 in 492f — similar, but...)
3. The scheduler is near the TOP of its range, not the middle — more headroom
4. Critically: **sigma was already on an upward trajectory when noise hit**, and the
   entropy gradient is strong enough to maintain it through the perturbation

In 492f, sigma growth was decelerating when noise hit (0.003/kstep). In 36700, sigma
growth was accelerating (0.005/kstep at 48-60k). The momentum carried through.

### The remaining schedule is punishing

Even if recovery holds, the policy faces in the next 40k steps:
- Random delay completion (70-90k)
- Dropout ramp (80-100k)
- Dynamics randomization (90-110k)
- Burst dropout (100-120k)
- FP/FN onset (100k, gentle ramp)

That's 5 overlapping perturbations while recovering from noise shock. In 928b's schedule,
these were spaced with 20k gaps. Here they overlap heavily.

## 5. Decision

### Continue running — this is the first viable recovery trajectory

No previous run recovered from a noise cliff. Even if the recovery stalls, the data
through 120k will be invaluable for understanding how the compressed schedule interacts
with sigma dynamics.

**Decision point at 100k**: If pair_valid > 0.60 and sigma > 0.60, the gentle FP/FN
ramp (100k-200k) has a chance. If pair_valid < 0.40 or sigma < 0.50, abort.

### What this experiment tests

1. Can the policy recover from noise shock with LR at max and sigma growing?
2. Does the compressed schedule's overlapping perturbation schedule overwhelm recovery?
3. Does sigma reach a high enough level for FP/FN onset at 100k?

### If this fails — next lever

If the compressed schedule still can't get sigma high enough before FP/FN:
- **Increase `entropy_loss_scale`** from 0.01 to 0.02 — directly accelerate sigma growth
- This is the only remaining lever that attacks the root cause (sigma too low)
  without restructuring the curriculum further
