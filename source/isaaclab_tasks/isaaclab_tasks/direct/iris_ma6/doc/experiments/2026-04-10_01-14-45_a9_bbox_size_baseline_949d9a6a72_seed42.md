## 4. Analysis

### Why sigma stayed low despite healthy LR

The `learning_epochs=4` fix prevented LR collapse but may have contributed to sigma
stagnation. Fewer epochs means:
- Less overfitting per update → more stable training (good)
- Less gradient signal per update → policy changes less per rollout (mixed)
- Entropy coefficient (0.01) has less influence with fewer gradient steps → weaker
  pressure toward exploration (bad)

However, 928b also used `c_entropy = 0.01` with 6 epochs and achieved sigma 1.2.
The difference is likely the **action space scaling**: with 2-4x wider physical effects
per unit action, the policy finds a narrow high-reward strategy faster and commits to it.
In 928b's gentler action space, more exploration was needed to find good strategies,
naturally keeping sigma higher.

### The sigma-robustness link

This experiment proves the causal chain:
```
Low sigma (0.44) → narrow action distribution → no handling of observation corruption
                 → FN misses cause target loss → tracking truncation cascade
                 → episode length collapses → reward signal disappears → no recovery
```

928b at the same curriculum milestone had sigma 1.22 — a broad action distribution that
included strategies for "bbox empty" states. When noise hit, 928b's pair_valid dipped
from 0.89 to 0.78 (−12%) and recovered. 949d's pair_valid collapsed from 0.85 to 0.20 (−76%).

### The entropy loss signature

Entropy loss was positive from step 8k onward (except brief negative at 4k). In 928b,
entropy loss was **negative** through 80k, meaning the entropy bonus was actively pushing
sigma higher. The identical entropy coefficient (0.01) produces opposite effects because
the reward landscape differs — in 949d's wider action space, the exploitation gradient
dominates the entropy gradient at every step.

stronger entropy gradient signal that pushes sigma upward.

## 5. Decision

### What worked
- `min_lr=3e-4` successfully prevented early LR collapse (the 20k→24k cliff is gone)
- Training quality through 100k was excellent (reward 3794, triangulation 28.6)

### What failed
- `learning_epochs=4` reduced KL (intended) but also reduced entropy-driven sigma growth
  (unintended). Sigma plateaued at 0.44 instead of reaching 928b's 1.2.
- The low-sigma policy couldn't survive noise/FP/FN onset at 100k.

### Next experiment options (in order of preference)

**Option A: `min_lr=3e-4` + `learning_epochs=6` + `kl_threshold=0.03`**
Revert epochs to 6 (for stronger entropy signal) but widen the dead zone from [0.01, 0.04]
to [0.015, 0.06] so the higher per-update KL doesn't trigger scheduler cuts. This
combines the LR floor fix with 928b-like epoch count. Risk: 928b showed kl_threshold=0.04
caused sigma divergence, but that was with min_lr=1e-4; the higher floor may stabilize it.

**Option B: `min_lr=3e-4` + `learning_epochs=4` + `entropy_loss_scale=0.03`**
Keep 4 epochs but triple the entropy bonus (0.01→0.03) to directly counteract the
exploitation gradient that's suppressing sigma. This is a targeted fix for the specific
failure mode (entropy loss positive = entropy too weak). Risk: too-high entropy may slow
convergence or create erratic policies.

**Option C: `min_lr=3e-4` + `learning_epochs=5`**
Split the difference. 5 epochs gives ~20% more gradient signal than 4 (including entropy
pressure) while still being 17% less cumulative KL than 6. Simplest change, lowest risk,
but may not be enough to close the sigma gap.

**Recommended: Option A.** The evidence suggests 6 epochs is needed for sigma growth, and
the real problem with 3461 was `min_lr` not `learning_epochs`. The `kl_threshold=0.03`
widening is conservative (928b's failure was at 0.04) and directly addresses the wider
action space's higher base KL.
